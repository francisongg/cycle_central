The rainy season here runs roughly from June to October, and typhoons can bring heavy rain well into November. Rain itself won't hurt a bike — grit, standing water and rust will. A few minutes of care after wet rides keeps your bike quiet, safe and lasting for years.

## After every wet ride

1. **Rinse off the grit.** Use a gentle stream of water or a bucket and sponge — never a pressure washer, which forces water into bearings.
2. **Dry it.** Wipe the frame, then bounce the bike a few times to shake water out of the frame and seatpost.
3. **Dry and lube the chain.** Wipe the chain with a rag, let it dry, then add one drop of lube per link and wipe off the excess. A dry chain rusts overnight.

That's it. Ten minutes, and your drivetrain lasts much longer.

## Chain and gears

In the rainy season, a **wet lube** stays on the chain longer than dry lube. It attracts more dirt, so clean the chain every week or two with a degreaser. If shifting gets slow or noisy, the chain or cable probably needs attention — don't wait for it to skip under load.

## Brakes wear faster in the wet

Grit turns brake pads into sandpaper. Check your pads every couple of weeks:

- **Rim brakes:** replace the pads when the grooves are gone, and wipe the rims clean.
- **Disc brakes:** if they squeal or feel weak, clean the rotors with isopropyl alcohol and a clean rag. Replace pads when the material is thinner than about 1 mm.

Remember that every bike takes longer to stop in the rain. Brake earlier and more gently, especially downhill.

## Tires and grip

Wet roads hide glass and sharp stones. Check your tires for cuts after rides, and pick out anything stuck in the tread. Many riders run slightly **lower tire pressure** in the wet for more grip — stay within the range printed on the tire. If you're tired of fixing flats, [tubeless tires](/blog/tubeless-tires-explained/) seal most small punctures on their own.

## Avoid flood water

Riding through deep water pushes dirty water into the hubs, bottom bracket and headset, where it causes rust and grinding. If you can't avoid a flooded street, walk the bike through if you can. If your bike has been submerged, bring it in for a check: the bearings may need fresh grease.

## E-bikes in the rain

Most e-bikes are **water-resistant, not waterproof**. Riding in rain is usually fine; submerging the motor or battery is not. Never pressure-wash an e-bike, dry the battery contacts before charging, and always charge indoors in a dry place.

## Be seen

Rain and dark clouds cut visibility for drivers. Use **front and rear lights even during the day**, and wear something bright or reflective. Fenders keep road spray off you and off the riders behind you.

## Storage

Keep your bike indoors or under a roof, out of the rain. A bike left wet overnight rusts at the chain, bolts and cables first. Once a month, check bolts are tight, look for rust spots and touch them up with a little lube.
