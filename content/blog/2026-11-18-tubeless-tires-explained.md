Tubeless tires have gone from mountain bikes to almost every kind of bike. If you're tired of roadside flats, they're worth a look. Here's how they work, what they're good at, and the catches nobody mentions.

## What "tubeless" means

A tubeless setup has **no inner tube**. The tire seals directly against the rim, and a few scoops of **liquid sealant** inside the tire plug small holes as soon as they happen. Most of the time, you won't even notice you've had a puncture.

## Why riders switch

- **Fewer flats.** Thorns, small glass and wire usually seal on their own within a few turns of the wheel. And with no tube, there's nothing to pinch when you hit a pothole.
- **More grip and comfort.** You can run lower pressure without pinch flats, so the tire grips better and soaks up rough roads. This is the biggest win on gravel and trails.
- **A little faster.** Without a tube rubbing inside the tire, rolling resistance drops slightly at the same pressure.

## The catches

- **Your rims and tires must be tubeless-ready.** Look for "tubeless ready", "TLR" or "TL" on both. The rims also need tubeless tape and valves.
- **Sealant needs topping up.** It slowly dries out — faster in our heat — so check it every 2–3 months and add more when you can't hear it sloshing.
- **Big cuts still need a fix.** A large gash won't seal. Carry a tire plug kit, and a spare tube just in case.
- **Setup can be messy.** Seating a new tire sometimes needs a burst of air from a booster pump or compressor. We can help with this.

## What you need

1. Tubeless-ready **rims** with tubeless **rim tape**
2. Tubeless-ready **tires**
3. Tubeless **valves**
4. **Sealant** (and a way to inject it, through the valve or before the last bit of the tire goes on)
5. A good **floor pump**, or a booster pump for stubborn tires

## Road, gravel or mountain?

- **Mountain bikes:** tubeless is the norm. Lower pressures and fewer pinch flats make a huge difference on trails.
- **Gravel bikes:** a near-perfect match. Wide tires at low pressure, and fewer flats far from home.
- **Road bikes:** increasingly common. Commuters and long-distance riders love the puncture protection; check your wheels are tubeless-ready first.

## Tire pressure: start lower than you think

There's no single right number — it depends on your weight, the tire width and the road. Start in the middle of the range printed on the tire, then drop a little at a time until the ride feels smooth without the tire squirming in corners. On **hookless rims**, never go above 5 bar (about 72 psi).

## When to stick with tubes

If your rims aren't tubeless-ready, you ride rarely (sealant dries out between rides), or you just want the simplest setup, good tires with inner tubes are still perfectly fine.
